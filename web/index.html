The experiment

Do recent changes
add useful information?

Compare recent results, current pitches, and recent changes. The question is whether tracking a change improves the next-month forecast beyond knowing what a pitcher throws now.

Definitions, coverage, and limitations

A · Results. Shrunk recent and historical strikeout and walk rates, workload, role, and opponent context. B · Current pitches. A plus last-30-day pitch measurements and usage. C · Recent changes. B plus changes from an earlier window.

Eligibility requires at least 50 batters faced in the preceding 60 days and an appearance in the preceding 14. Forecasts cover the following 30 calendar days. Pitch measurements compare the last 30 days with days 31–120, or the prior season when the earlier window contains fewer than 150 pitches.

Alerts require a C-minus-B difference of at least two percentage points in projected K% minus BB%, at least 150 current and comparison pitches, no repertoire fallback, and a 30-day cooldown per pitcher. With at least 50 later batters faced, an alert is “confirmed” only if the observed K% minus BB% differs from B by at least two points in the alerted direction. Smaller follow-up samples are unresolved. These outcomes do not establish a change in underlying skill.

“Already evident” compares recent K% minus BB% with the prior-season rate, requiring at least 50 batters faced in each. It is a descriptive check, not proof of lead time. Missing past exposure is shown separately.

Weekly outcome windows overlap. Model-comparison intervals resample whole pitchers and do not fully capture shared calendar effects. Upcoming opponents use the schedule of games actually played. Repertoire flags check each current usage share separately, not support for every combination of traits.

These are historical point forecasts, not live projections or causal coaching recommendations. Individual forecast intervals are not estimated. The model and alert rules were fixed before the 2025 evaluation; the holdout is now used.
